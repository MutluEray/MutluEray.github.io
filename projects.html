2025 – 2026

Context-Aware Speller

A context-aware assistive speller designed for individuals with severe speech and motor impairments (e.g. ALS). Unlike traditional letter-by-letter spellers, it listens to the conversational partner, obtains user approval of the context, and then generates AI-powered suggestions at letter, word and full-sentence levels. The user retains full control and selects via gaze (or other pointing methods). Early evaluation on 50 real-life communication scenarios showed approximately 1.7× faster response times (≈40 % reduction) compared with a conventional eye-tracking speller operating at ~15 words per minute.

SSVEP Detection in BCIs

Compared multiple machine-learning pipelines for Steady-State Visual Evoked Potential (SSVEP) detection from 64-channel EEG (35 participants). The optimized Extra Trees classifier achieved the highest accuracy (82.5 %) and consistently strong performance across confusion matrices, ROC-AUC and precision-recall metrics.

The network samples 3D patches biased toward lesion tissue during training, then reconstructs full-volume predictions at evaluation time via overlap-averaged sliding-window inference.

Interpretability vs. Explainability in Stroke Outcome Prediction

A head-to-head comparison of two paradigms for trusting a clinical model: inherently interpretable models (logistic regression, a shallow decision tree) versus post-hoc explanations (SHAP, permutation importance) applied to a black-box gradient-boosted model — trained on identical baseline clinical features from the International Stroke Trial to predict 6-month death/dependency. Beyond just running SHAP on a black box, as most clinical XAI papers do, this project directly tests whether the two paradigms agree, whether the post-hoc explanation is faithful to what the model actually does (against a random-order baseline), and whether it's reproducible across resampled training data.

Sleep Stage Classification on Sleep-EDF

Built an end-to-end pipeline for automatic 5-class sleep-stage scoring (Wake, N1, N2, N3, REM) from polysomnography (EEG, EOG, EMG) on the Sleep-EDF Expanded dataset (22 patients). Engineered ~900 time/frequency-domain features per 30s epoch via tsflex, tsfresh, antropy, and YASA band power, with strictly patient-grouped cross-validation throughout — no patient's epochs ever appear in both train and test within a fold. A gradient-boosted model (CatBoost) was tuned with early stopping and statistical feature selection to close an initial overfitting gap, but a simpler linear baseline matched it on accuracy while generalizing better.

Optical Triangulation for Imaging Sulcus Vocalis

Designed a fiber-optic probe concept for detecting sulcus vocalis — a structural vocal-fold abnormality that current laryngoscopy methods struggle to image reliably — using optical triangulation instead of conventional endoscopic imaging. Derived the fiber-angle/cutting-angle relationship analytically from Snell's law and anatomical constraints (5–10 mm probe diameter, 25–50 mm target range), then verified it against ZEMAX non-sequential ray-trace simulations across multiple angle combinations, matching analytical and simulated results to within 20% error. Designed the probe body and angled fiber-polishing jigs in SolidWorks and Fusion 360, 3D-printed the jigs, and used them to polish real fiber-optic cable to the target angles, confirmed under microscope.

Seizure Onset as a Bifurcation: An Excitatory/Inhibitory Network Model

A simulation study that treats seizure onset as a dynamical transition instead of a detection problem. Starting from a published excitatory/inhibitory network (Kim & Nykamp, 2017; originally written for Brian 1), I ported it to Brian2 (3,750 conductance-based Morris–Lecar neurons) and tested whether interneurons that stop firing under strong input (depolarization block) give an abrupt, hysteretic and triggerable switch from a calm state to a seizure-like state as the external drive is ramped up and back down. At a fixed drive the network sits in either state depending on its history, and a 100 ms pulse moves it from one to the other for good. A two-population rate model built from the measured neuron responses reproduces the two stable states and the unstable state between them, and removing the interneuron shutdown removes most of the bistable window. Adding a slow adaptation variable makes the high state switch itself off and repeat.

Explainable Knowledge-Graph Drug Repurposing for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's Disease

A pipeline that ranks drug-repurposing hypotheses for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease on Hetionet (47,031 nodes, 2.25M edges) and shows the evidence behind each one. Degree-weighted path counts over three compound-to-disease metapaths are combined with a z-score against 100 degree-preserving permutations of the graph, so the ranking is not simply a list of promiscuous drugs. Each shortlisted candidate is explained by its actual supporting paths, retrieved with SPARQL (rdflib) from an RDF export of the graph, and audited with gene-level counterfactuals: remove the gene driving a candidate and see where it lands. On a 58-disease benchmark the consensus score reaches AUROC 0.76 against 0.70 for a "number of bound genes" baseline.

Does a Better-Calibrated fMRI Model Reproduce Better? A From-Scratch GLM Audit

Implemented a first-level and group-level fMRI general linear model from scratch in NumPy — design matrix construction, AR(1) noise prewhitening, group inference — and validated it against nilearn and against simulations with known ground truth. Found that standard per-voxel AR(1) prewhitening carries a small anticonservative bias (present in nilearn's own implementation too), implemented a documented correction, and confirmed the effect on two independent public datasets. Preprocessed raw multi-session data with fMRIPrep and tested whether better statistical calibration also means a more reproducible activation map across scanning sessions. It doesn't, cleanly: whole-brain-corrected significance can differ sharply between two sessions even where the underlying response looks similar, consistent with the low test-retest reliability widely reported for task-fMRI.

Same subject, same task, same statistical threshold and color scale, two scanning sessions ~6 months apart. The peak response near right precentral/postcentral cortex looks comparable in both sessions here — but after correcting for ~228,000 whole-brain comparisons, only the top session's cluster survived at FDR q<.05 (314 voxels vs. 4).

Test-retest reliability benchmark: Elliott et al., Psychological Science 2020 (mean ICC ≈ 0.40 across 90 task-fMRI studies), with commentary from Kragel et al., 2021. Prewhitening bias correction follows Worsley et al., NeuroImage 2002.

Diabetes Prediction with Exploratory Data Analysis

Developed a complete machine-learning pipeline for early prediction of diabetes mellitus on the PIMA Indian Diabetes Dataset (768 patients, 8 clinical features). After thorough exploratory data analysis, feature engineering and normalization, five supervised algorithms were trained and compared: Naïve Bayes, Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machine and an Adaptive Boosting ensemble. With optimized hyperparameters, KNN achieved the highest performance (accuracy and F1 ≈ 0.99 on the held-out test set).

How Much Autonomy Does a Sparse BCI Channel Need? Hierarchical Latent Planning for Assistive Manipulation

A simulation study of how much robot autonomy a noisy, low-bandwidth BCI intent channel needs. A simulated user repeats one high-level command (which disc, which zone) through a decoder with a given accuracy; a Bayesian belief over the possible intents commits once it is confident; and a CAVIN-inspired two-level latent planner (effect codes and motion codes, trained on self-supervised interaction data in MuJoCo, planning with CEM/MPC) fills in the multi-step manipulation. I compare it with direct low-level control (four directions plus grip, same noisy decoder) and with a scripted oracle executor that separates decoding errors from planning errors. The planner needs 1–5 commands per task instead of 31–71, and succeeds more often than direct control when decoder accuracy is about 0.7 or lower; above about 0.9, direct control succeeds more often, and the planner trails the oracle throughout. It also fails at pushing, which I report together with four attempted fixes that did not work. One round of on-policy data aggregation raised success from 0.78/0.73 to 0.86/0.80 (unblocked/blocked, fresh scenes).

The BCI signal is simulated, not EEG: a discrete command vocabulary with a confusion matrix set by decoder accuracy. The arm is cosmetic, grasping is a kinematic weld, observations are low-dimensional states, and there is no user study. The write-up spells out what that does and does not support.

Method follows the two-level latent planning idea of Fang et al., CoRL 2019 (CAVIN), re-implemented on low-dimensional states rather than reproduced. Task-level hierarchical BCI control with noisy commands is an established idea (for example Chung et al., IJCAI 2011); this study contributes a controlled simulation sweep of success and user input against decoder accuracy, and documented negative results. Limitations: simulated decoder and user, abstract manipulation, planner reliability below the oracle, pushing unsolved, no user study.
