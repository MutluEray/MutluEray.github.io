2024 – Present Industry collaboration

Early Prediction of Driver Drowsiness Using Ear-EEG

Fatigue is a major contributor to road accidents. This project, conducted in collaboration with Cebreo Medical, explores the feasibility of detecting driver drowsiness from in-ear electroencephalography (ear-EEG) — a more wearable alternative to conventional scalp EEG. We collected ethics-approved data from human participants during simulated driving sessions and applied unsupervised clustering (K-means, Gaussian Mixture Models, DBSCAN) on spectral features, including novel composite metrics. K-means achieved a Silhouette score of 0.93, with the new metrics outperforming traditional band-power ratios in separating alert and drowsy states. The results support a practical pathway toward real-time, label-free fatigue monitoring systems that can be integrated into everyday hearables.

Related: Clustering-Based Detection of Driver Drowsiness with Ear-EEG — 14th International Conference on Brain–Computer Interface (BCI), 2026.

2025 – Present Explainable AI

Interpreting AI ECG Models via Physiology-Inspired Counterfactual Explanations

Deep learning models for electrocardiogram (ECG) analysis often achieve high performance but remain opaque to clinicians. This work develops counterfactual explanation methods that generate physiologically meaningful modifications to single-lead, single-beat ECG signals. By showing how small, clinically interpretable changes alter the model’s prediction, the approach aims to increase trust and support clinical adoption of AI-ECG systems. The methods are designed to align with known cardiac physiology rather than purely data-driven attributions.

Accepted at IEEE EMBC 2026 (first-author).
