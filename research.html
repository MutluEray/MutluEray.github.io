2024 – Present

Early Prediction of Driver Drowsiness Using Ear-EEG

Fatigue remains a leading factor in road accidents. In collaboration with Cebreo Medical we investigated the feasibility of detecting driver drowsiness from in-ear electroencephalography (ear-EEG), a more wearable alternative to conventional scalp EEG. We extracted spectral features (including novel composite metrics) and applied unsupervised clustering (K-means, GMM, DBSCAN). K-means achieved a Silhouette score of 0.93; the new metrics outperformed traditional band-power ratios in separating alert and drowsy states. The results demonstrate a practical path toward real-time, label-free fatigue monitoring systems that can be integrated into everyday hearables.

Mutlu, E., Saibene, M., de Araujo, J., Ahrens, E., & Puthusserypady, S. Clustering-Based Detection of Driver Drowsiness with Ear-EEG. 14th International Conference on Brain–Computer Interface (BCI), 2026.

2025 – Present

Interpreting AI ECG Models via Physiology-Inspired Counterfactual Explanations

Deep learning models for electrocardiogram analysis often reach high performance but remain opaque to clinicians. This work develops physiology-inspired counterfactual explanations for single-lead, single-beat ECG classification. By generating small, clinically meaningful modifications that change the model’s prediction, the method reveals which morphological and temporal features drive decisions. The goal is to increase trust and support clinical adoption of AI-ECG systems by aligning explanations with known cardiac physiology rather than purely data-driven attributions.

Mutlu, E.*, Shen, D.*, Hempel, P., Brink-Kjær, A., You, L., & Spicher, N. Interpreting AI ECG Models via Physiology-Inspired Counterfactual Explanations. Accepted at IEEE EMBC, 2026.

2022 – 2023

Digital Stethoscope Using TinyML

Designed and implemented an embedded digital stethoscope that classifies organ sounds (heart, lung, bowel) in real time on a resource-constrained device. A custom convolutional neural network (FISC-Net) first identifies the auscultated organ and then applies organ-specific digital filtering. Quantization-aware training reduced model size by a factor of four while preserving approximately 90 % accuracy. The system bridges classical auscultation with edge AI, enabling portable, low-power analysis that keeps computation on-device and thereby preserves patient privacy.

Mutlu, E., Hüseyin, V., & Serbes, G. Bridging Auscultation and Tiny Machine Learning. Traitement du Signal, 2024.
Mutlu, E., Hüseyin, V., & Serbes, G. FISC-Net: Classification of Auscultation Sounds on an Edge Device. 2023 IEEE IMSA.
