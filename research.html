Research

Research on transparent, deployable AI for biosignals, carried out with academic and industry collaborators.

Models that sense, forecast and act on signals from the brain and body are only useful if they hold up for people they have never seen, run where people are, and show why they reached a decision. This research builds and tests such models, from wearable sensing to closed-loop neurotechnology.

  • Wearables
  • Biosignal forecasting
  • Transparent AI
  • Embedded and edge machine learning
  • Brain–computer interfaces and closed-loop neurotechnology
  • Evaluation that generalises across people and sites

Driver drowsiness detection and forecasting

Fatigue remains a leading factor in road accidents. In collaboration with Cebreo Medical we investigated the feasibility of detecting driver drowsiness from in-ear electroencephalography (ear-EEG), a more wearable alternative to conventional scalp EEG. An ethics-approved human study was conducted during simulated driving. We extracted established spectral features and composite metrics and applied unsupervised clustering (K-means, GMM, DBSCAN). K-means achieved a Silhouette score of 0.93; the composite metrics outperformed simple band-power ratios in separating alert and drowsy states. The results demonstrate a practical path toward real-time, label-free fatigue monitoring systems that can be integrated into everyday hearables.

Detection flags drowsiness only once it has begun. EEG-REFORM extends the work to forecasting: it forecasts future EEG representations and classifies the forecast state, so a warning can come before vigilance drops. It is evaluated on the public SEED-VIG and SADT datasets with leave-one-subject-out cross-validation.

Physiology-inspired counterfactual explanations for ECG models

Deep learning models for electrocardiogram analysis often reach high performance but remain opaque to clinicians. This work develops physiology-inspired counterfactual explanations for single-lead, single-beat ECG classification. By generating small, clinically meaningful modifications that change the model’s prediction, the method reveals which morphological and temporal features drive decisions. The counterfactual edits matched clinical criteria for left and right bundle branch block (QRS widening) and atrioventricular block (PR prolongation), aligning explanations with known cardiac physiology rather than purely data-driven attributions.

Ongoing: multi-beat counterfactuals for wearable as well as hospital ECG, and explainability of ECG foundation models.

A digital stethoscope with on-device machine learning

Designed and implemented an embedded digital stethoscope that classifies organ sounds (heart, lungs and bowel) in real time on a resource-constrained device. A custom convolutional neural network (FISC-Net) first identifies the auscultated organ and then applies organ-specific digital filtering. Quantization-aware training reduced model size by a factor of four while preserving approximately 90 % accuracy. The system bridges classical auscultation with edge AI, enabling portable, low-power analysis that keeps computation on-device and thereby preserves patient privacy. Data were acquired with ethical approval.
