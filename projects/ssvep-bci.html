High-Speed SSVEP Speller: Benchmarking Detectors on a 40-Target BCI

Three established SSVEP detectors compared on a 40-target speller benchmark: 98.3% accuracy from 2 s of EEG, about 229 bits/min at best.

Data
Tsinghua benchmark (35 participants)

Steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEPs) power the fastest non-invasive brain–computer interface spellers: each key flickers at its own frequency, and the visual cortex echoes the frequency the user looks at. The hard part is telling 40 targets only 0.2 Hz apart from a fraction of a second of EEG. On the public Tsinghua benchmark (35 participants, 64-channel EEG), I implemented and compared three established detectors: training-free filter-bank CCA, extended CCA with user templates, and ensemble task-related component analysis (TRCA). Each was evaluated by training on five blocks of a participant's data and testing on the sixth, at data lengths from 0.3 to 2 s, with paired statistics across participants.

Right: occipital EEG with the analysis window, and the score for each of the 40 targets. Key flicker is slowed 8× for display (real stimuli run at 8–15.8 Hz).

Speed versus accuracy

35 participants, mean ± SEM. A few minutes of a user's own calibration data lifts accuracy at 0.5 s from 25% to 81%. Ensemble TRCA gives the best trade-off, significantly ahead of extended CCA at every window up to 0.8 s (paired Wilcoxon, Holm-corrected); above 1 s all calibrated methods exceed 94%.

Methods: Chen et al., J. Neural Eng. 2015 (FBCCA) and PNAS 2015 (extended CCA); Nakanishi et al., IEEE TBME 2018 (TRCA). Data: Wang et al., IEEE TNSRE 2017.
