Do Deep Vision Models Predict Human EEG? A Brain-Score-Style Layer Analysis on THINGS-EEG2

Which layers of ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 predict human EEG responses to images, and how much of that is low-level image structure?

Year
2021
Data
THINGS-EEG2 (9 subjects)
Methods
Encoding models, noise ceilings, two-way bootstrap
Status
Re-analysed and written up in 2026

A Brain-Score-style analysis adapted from primate recordings to human EEG. For every layer of ImageNet-trained ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16, a cross-validated ridge regression maps the layer’s activations to the EEG response to the same image (THINGS-EEG2: 9 subjects, 17 occipito-parietal channels, 16,540 training and 200 held-out test images). Predictivity is scored on the held-out images and divided by the EEG’s own split-half reliability. To see what “learned” adds, every comparison includes an untrained ResNet-50 (with calibrated normalization) and a non-learned Gabor-energy + Fourier-power baseline. Uncertainty comes from a two-way bootstrap over subjects and test images, and each model’s layer is chosen leave-one-subject-out. Because readout choices moved the first results, I added a spatial-pooling sensitivity analysis and report where the conclusions change.

  • Trained networks predict EEG far better than an untrained network (+0.21 normalized r overall), and the advantage is larger after 200 ms (+0.24) than before (+0.17).
  • An untrained ResNet-50 is statistically indistinguishable from a Gabor/Fourier baseline: much of the early predictivity is low-level image structure.
  • The depth profile depends on the metric: the sharp early peak favours early-to-mid layers, sustained late predictivity favours deeper ones.
  • ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 are comparable. How the ViT is read out matters more (≈ +0.05 for a spatial vs. mean-token readout), and the early-window sign of their difference flips at the finest readout.

Only two supervised architectures were tested, so this says nothing general about CNNs versus transformers.

Data: THINGS-EEG2, Gifford et al., NeuroImage 2022 (CC-BY 4.0), authors’ preprocessed epochs. Method follows the Brain-Score idea (Schrimpf et al., 2018, 2020). The analysis adds untrained and low-level baselines, a noise-ceiling normalization, an image-level bootstrap and a readout-sensitivity analysis. Limitations: 9 subjects and 200 test images, 17 channels, overlapping responses in the 5 Hz image stream, and layer-selection uncertainty is not propagated into the intervals.
