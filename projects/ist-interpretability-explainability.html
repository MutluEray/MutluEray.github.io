Interpretability vs. Explainability in Stroke Outcome Prediction

Interpretable models against post-hoc explanations for 19,435 stroke patients: do the two agree, and are the explanations stable?

Code on GitHub
Year
2023
Data
International Stroke Trial (19,435 patients)
Methods
SHAP, permutation importance, interpretable baselines
Status
Re-analysed and written up in 2026

A head-to-head comparison of two paradigms for trusting a clinical model: inherently interpretable models (logistic regression, a shallow decision tree) versus post-hoc explanations (SHAP, permutation importance) applied to a black-box gradient-boosted model — trained on identical baseline clinical features from the International Stroke Trial to predict 6-month death/dependency. Rather than only running SHAP on a black box, this project tests whether the two paradigms agree, whether the post-hoc explanation is faithful to what the model actually does (against a random-order baseline), and whether it's reproducible across resampled training data.

Compare Feature Importance Across Methods

Same trained models, four ways of asking "what matters" — pick a clinical variable and see how much each method thinks it drives the prediction. Bars are normalized to each method's own strongest feature overall.

Design directly tests the argument in Rudin, Nat. Mach. Intell. 2019 (prefer interpretable models when they match black-box accuracy), and measures the disagreement problem (Krishna et al., 2022) directly on clinical data.
