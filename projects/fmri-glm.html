Does a Better-Calibrated fMRI Model Reproduce Better? A From-Scratch GLM Audit

An fMRI GLM written from scratch and validated against nilearn, asking whether better calibration also means better test–retest reproducibility.

Code on GitHub
Year
2022
Methods
GLM, AR(1) prewhitening, fMRIPrep
Status
Re-analysed and written up in 2026

Implemented a first-level and group-level fMRI general linear model from scratch in NumPy — design matrix construction, AR(1) noise prewhitening, group inference — and validated it against nilearn and against simulations with known ground truth. Found that per-voxel AR(1) prewhitening left a small anticonservative bias in these data, also with nilearn's AR(1) option, consistent with earlier reports that AR(1) noise models are often too simple for fMRI; implemented a documented correction and confirmed the effect on two independent public datasets. Preprocessed raw multi-session data with fMRIPrep and tested whether better statistical calibration also means a more reproducible activation map across scanning sessions. It doesn't, cleanly: whole-brain-corrected significance can differ sharply between two sessions even where the underlying response looks similar, consistent with the low test-retest reliability widely reported for task-fMRI.

Same subject, same task, same statistical threshold and color scale, two scanning sessions ~6 months apart. The peak response near right precentral/postcentral cortex looks comparable in both sessions here — but after correcting for ~228,000 whole-brain comparisons, only the top session's cluster survived at FDR q<.05 (314 voxels vs. 4).

Test-retest reliability benchmark: Elliott et al., Psychological Science 2020 (mean ICC ≈ 0.40 across 90 task-fMRI studies), with commentary from Kragel et al., 2021. Prewhitening bias correction follows Worsley et al., NeuroImage 2002; on AR(1) misspecification, see Olszowy et al., Nat. Commun. 2019.
