Can Physics Tell You Where to Put a Wearable EEG Electrode?

Tests whether a head model can choose where a two-electrode wearable should go, checked against real scalp, around-ear and in-ear EEG from 15 people.

Code on GitHub Read the full write-up
Year
2024
Data
Ear-SAAD (15 participants)
Methods
MNE forward models, closed-form decoding, leave-one-subject-out
Status
Re-analysed and written up in 2026

A wearable has room for two electrodes, not a cap. Choosing where they go is usually done by picking channels from a full cap recording, which can only choose among positions the cap measured. A head model can evaluate any position. This study tests whether its predictions hold up against real recordings, on a public dataset with scalp, around-ear and in-ear EEG recorded at the same time (15 participants, auditory attention decoding). Each virtual electrode is a minimum-norm source estimate from the scalp channels, projected forward to any point on the skin (MNE, fsaverage, 3-layer BEM). Every step had a pre-specified prediction or go/no-go criterion. All ~110,000 candidate pairs on the scalp were scored with a closed-form version of the decoder, and the one-layout-for-everyone choice was tested leave-one-subject-out.

  • Where electrodes exist, simple interpolation is enough; where they don't, physics helps. Predicting around-ear EEG from the scalp alone, the head model reaches 0.29 against 0.12 for interpolation, about 80 % of the best any linear method achieves (0.37).
  • But it predicts the signal better than the decoding ranking. Around the ear it ranks electrode pairs for attention decoding at ρ = 0.34 after calibration, just below the pre-specified 0.35, so off-cap layouts still need a recording. On the cap, with real pairs removed and predicted, it ranks 55 pairs at 0.68 (interpolation 0.80, noise ceiling 0.89).
  • The best spot is broad and robust. It sits over left centro-parietal scalp around CP5. It barely moves when skull and scalp conductivity change, and it moves about 1 cm (under 2 % decoding loss) when the cap electrodes are mis-registered by about 1 cm.
  • Miniaturisation costs depend on electrode noise. With noise-free virtual electrodes, 1–3 cm pairs lose nothing; with realistic electrode noise they lose 21–33 %.

Scope: accuracies for model-placed pairs come from physics-predicted signals plus modelled electrode noise, not from a device. Virtual electrodes are combinations of the 29 recorded scalp channels, so the model can say where a small device captures what the cap sees, not find information the cap missed. Template head (no pinna, no ear canal), one task, 15 participants. Five of the twelve pre-specified predictions failed; the write-up lists all twelve.
